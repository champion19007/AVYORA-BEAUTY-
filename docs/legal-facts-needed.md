# Facts needed before the shop can take a rupee

Every `[TO CONFIRM]` in the legal pages is a fact only the business owner has.
They were left as markers rather than filled with plausible-looking text
because a policy that states the wrong registered address or an invented
grievance officer is worse than one that visibly has a blank — the blank gets
fixed, the confident lie does not.

There are 23 markers across five pages, but only **14 distinct facts**. Fill
these in and every page resolves.

Once answered, the values go into the pages in one pass and this file is
deleted.

## Two decisions worth making deliberately

**Return window on cosmetics.** For hygiene reasons an opened skincare product
usually cannot be resold. Most Indian D2C brands accept returns only on unopened
items, and handle opened ones as a goodwill replacement rather than a right.
Whatever you choose, the page has to say it plainly — a vague policy is decided
against you.

**Who pays return shipping.** At roughly ₹70–100 a parcel this is not a
rounding error once volume arrives. Saying nothing means arguing it case by
case forever.

## Not a `[TO CONFIRM]`, but on the same blocker list

Cosmetics sold in India need a manufacturing licence under the Cosmetics Rules
2020. If a third party manufactures for you they hold it, but your labels must
carry the correct manufacturer details, and Legal Metrology rules require net
quantity, MRP, batch number and expiry. This outranks everything above: a
labelling failure is a recall, not a correction.
